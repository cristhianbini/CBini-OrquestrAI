# Evaluate OrquestrAI in 10 minutes

🇺🇸 **English** · 🇧🇷 [Português](eval-br.md) · [README](../README.md)

Not an installation guide — a route for engineers who want to judge the idea, and tell us where it breaks.

1. **The thesis** — agents are engines; OrquestrAI is the layer that decides how their work becomes real. [Technical overview](arch.md#the-thesis)
2. **Two planes** — where people and agents decide (control) versus where changes happen (execution). [Two planes](arch.md#two-planes)
3. **The life of a change** — nine steps from intent to evidence, and the boundary at each one. [Life of a change](arch.md#the-life-of-a-change)
4. **The security model** — what is proven, what is in validation, what is assumed. [Security model](security.md)
5. **The limits we declare** — data boundary with cloud providers, revert scope, non-goals. [Limitations](security.md#limitations-and-non-goals)
6. **What actually works today** — and what does not yet. [Current maturity](../README.md#current-maturity)
7. **How we decide something is done** — design, automated proof, human proof, independent review, promotion. [Engineering evidence](arch.md#engineering-evidence)
8. **Challenge an assumption** — [Architecture review discussion](https://github.com/cristhianbini/CBini-OrquestrAI/discussions/2)
9. **Anything else** — ideas, UX or documentation through the issue templates. [How to take part](../CONTRIBUTING.md)
10. **Come back later** — see whether the critique changed the design or the roadmap. [Roadmap](roadmap.md)
